# Prompt 1: What is in the water from a fridge dispenser?

Paste everything below the line into a deep-research tool. Use the strongest model you have.

---

## Role

You are a careful research analyst with a background in drinking-water microbiology and premise plumbing. This is for an independent, open-science project and a video essay about refrigerator water dispensers. The author has a personal interest in the question. Treat that as a reason to be more careful, not less. **The goal is the truth, not confirmation.**

## The question

> **What do measurement studies show about the microbes, and the gases and by-products they make, in water from refrigerator dispensers, point-of-use carbon filters and similar appliances, compared with the tap water that feeds them? How does it change with filter age, stagnation, temperature and power cuts?**

This prompt is only about **exposure**: what is in the glass. Do not review health effects here.

## What to find

1. **Counts.** Heterotrophic plate counts (state the medium and incubation temperature, for example R2A at 20–22 °C or plate count agar at 35 °C) and total cell counts (flow cytometry). Report medians and ranges, and keep CFU and cells separate: they are not interchangeable.
2. **Organisms.** Which genera and species, found by culture or by sequencing, with attention to *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, non-tuberculous mycobacteria, *Legionella*, *Stenotrophomonas*, *Sphingomonas*, *Methylobacterium*, yeasts and moulds.
3. **Sulfate-reducing and sulphide-producing bacteria, and hydrogen sulfide.** Any measurement in point-of-use filters, dispensers, water coolers or household plumbing. Also any measurement of hydrogen sulfide or other volatile sulphur compounds in such water.
4. **Endotoxin** (EU/mL) in tap, filtered and dispenser water.
5. **What drives it.** Filter age and throughput, stagnation (first draw against flushed), temperature, power cuts, tubing material, silver-impregnated carbon, and the outlet (nozzle and drip tray) against the inside of the lines.
6. **In-fridge dispensers specifically,** against office water coolers, bottled-water coolers, mains-connected microfiltered dispensers and ice machines. Label which kind each study measured. Do not let cooler studies stand in for fridge studies.

## Where we are (leads to check; do not assume they are correct or complete)

Identifiers below were checked against PubMed or Crossref by the project. Read them, and find more.

- Swanson CS, He Q. In-refrigerator water dispensing systems and microbial contamination at the microbiome scale. *Am J Infect Control* 2024. PMID 38460731. The only in-fridge study found so far. Short report: find the sample size and methods.
- Zhou Z et al. Microbiota and antibiotic resistome in household activated-carbon purifiers. *Environ Int* 2021. PMID 33486296.
- Thomson RM et al. *Mycobacterium abscessus* and drinking water, including plumbed-in refrigerator taps. *Am J Respir Crit Care Med* 2025. PMID 40072241.
- Gomez-Smith CK et al. Sulfate-reducing bacteria and mycobacteria in drinking-water biofilm. *Environ Sci Technol* 2015. PMID 26098899.
- Payment P et al. Bacterial counts in household point-of-use filter units, and illness. *Appl Environ Microbiol* 1991. PMID 2059052.
- Water-dispenser studies: Liguori 2010 (DOI 10.1186/1471-2180-10-19); Zanetti 2009 (10.1016/j.ijfoodmicro.2008.10.007); Sacchetti 2014 (10.1016/j.ijheh.2013.06.002); Girolamini 2019 (10.3390/ijerph16020272); Baumgartner 2006 (10.4315/0362-028X-69.12.3043); Lévesque 1994 (10.1128/aem.60.4.1174-1178.1994).
- A 2025 review, Hile et al., *AIMS Microbiol* (PMID 41522444), lists about 70 dispenser studies. **Use its reference list, not its text:** some in-text attributions are wrong (one claim is cited to an unrelated paper).
- For scale: Lugli et al. 2022 (PMID 35355372) measured ordinary tap water in Parma at about 2.8 × 10⁴ cells/mL by flow cytometry.

## Rules of evidence

1. Every study needs a PMID or DOI, and you must have opened its record. Say whether you read the full text or only the abstract.
2. **Copy the title, first author, year and journal from the record itself.** Never from memory, and never from the leads above.
3. Quote the sentence that reports each key number. If you can't, mark the study UNVERIFIED and leave it out of your conclusions.
4. Report only searches you actually ran. Don't claim access to databases you couldn't use.
5. Vendor pages, filter-seller blogs and influencer content are leads only, never evidence.
6. Report low counts and null findings as carefully as high ones.
7. If you didn't find something, write "not found in these searches", never "doesn't exist".

## Output

1. **Direct answer** (150 words): what a fridge dispenser's water is known to contain, and how confident you are.
2. **Search log:** source, date, query, hits, kept.
3. **Measurement table:** study | appliance type | country | n units | sample type (first draw or flushed, water or swab) | method | counts (median, range, units) | organisms | H2S or sulphur bacteria | endotoxin.
4. **Drivers:** what filter age, stagnation, temperature, power cuts and outlet hygiene do, with sources.
5. **The working range:** the project assumes 10³ to 10⁵ CFU/mL heterotrophic bacteria for a neglected dispenser, at 0.25 to 2 L a day. Confirm, correct or replace it, with sources.
6. **Gaps:** what has never been measured in fridge dispensers, and how to measure it.
7. **Verification table:** identifier | title as on the record | first author | year | full text or abstract.
