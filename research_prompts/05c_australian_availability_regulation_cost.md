# Prompt 5c: Gut and dysbiosis-related testing in Australia: regulation, availability, cost

Paste everything below the line into a deep-research tool. Use the strongest model you have. (Run third of three follow-ups.)

---

## Role

You are a careful analyst in Australian health regulation and pathology services. The goal is to find out what is regulated, available, reimbursed and what it costs, using official sources, not to promote any test or provider. Report evidence against as readily as evidence for.

## Context

A previous review of dysbiosis biomarkers found that a recent international consensus advises against reporting dysbiosis indices, that consumer stool-sequencing tests disagreed with each other in a small comparison, and that only faecal calprotectin (gut inflammation) and blood phosphatidylethanol (alcohol exposure) are validated, for adjacent questions. **It could not find regulator positions, costs or availability.** "Not found in these searches" means a gap in the search, so say so plainly and never invent a figure.

## Find

1. **Regulation.** How the **Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA)** regulates in-vitro diagnostic devices and **consumer ("direct-to-consumer") microbiome and gut tests**; any TGA, ACCC or ASIC statements, warnings or enforcement on microbiome testing or "gut health" tests; how **NATA** accreditation and **NPAAC** standards apply; whether a test being non-accredited or "for research use" has to be disclosed.
2. **Professional positions.** Statements from the **Royal College of Pathologists of Australasia (RCPA)**, **Gastroenterological Society of Australia (GESA)**, **Dietitians Australia**, **RACGP**, **Australian Dental Association** or equivalent bodies on microbiome tests, faecal calprotectin, hydrogen and methane breath tests, intestinal-permeability tests and breath-sulfide measurement.
3. **Availability and who can order.** For faecal calprotectin, hydrogen and methane breath tests, phosphatidylethanol, cotinine, coeliac serology and routine blood tests: which can be ordered by a GP, which need a specialist, and which are offered by the large public and private pathology providers. Include any state public-pathology service in Western Australia.
4. **Cost and reimbursement.** Medicare Benefits Schedule item numbers and rebates (or the absence of a rebate), typical out-of-pocket cost ranges quoted by providers, and any published price lists. Give the date of each figure.
5. **Repeat intervals** recommended in Australian or major international guidelines for faecal calprotectin and breath testing.
6. **Direct-to-consumer tests sold in Australia:** which exist and any independent evaluation of them (not provider marketing).

## Rules

1. **Official sources first:** legislation, regulator and college websites, the MBS online, guidelines and peer-reviewed literature. Every figure and every claim about what is regulated or reimbursed needs a stable URL, a page title, the date accessed and, where shown, the date of the page. Say whether you read the page itself or only a search summary.
2. Provider and vendor pages are leads for availability only, never evidence for what a test shows. State the date of any price.
3. Quote the sentence that supports each key fact. If you can't, mark it UNVERIFIED and leave it out of your conclusions.
4. If you didn't find something, write "not found in these searches", never "does not exist" and never an estimate.

## Output

1. **Direct answer** (150 words): what is regulated, what is clearly available through a GP, what is not reimbursed, and the strongest caution.
2. **Regulation and positions:** table with body | statement or rule | date | URL | page read or summary only.
3. **Availability and cost table:** test | who can order | MBS item and rebate (or none) | out-of-pocket range | date of figure | source URL.
4. **Repeat intervals:** table with test | interval | guideline | URL.
5. **Direct-to-consumer tests in Australia:** what exists and any independent evaluation.
6. **Verification table:** URL or identifier | title | publisher | date | page read or summary only.

Describe what is regulated, available and priced. Do not suggest diagnoses or treatments for any individual.
