# plates/

Photos of the agar plates. Keep the plates **sealed and taped, and never open them.** Photograph through the lid, or through the base if the lid is fogged.

## File names

`<plate>-day<N>-<view>.jpg`, for example:

- `pilot-2-day4-whole.jpg` (the whole plate, from a phone or the microscope at its lowest zoom)
- `pilot-2-day4-z1-centre.jpg` (a microscope field, zoom setting 1, the centre of the plate)
- `pilot-2-day4-z1-colony-a.jpg` (a colony you want to look at more closely)
- `calibration-z1.jpg` (a millimetre ruler photographed at zoom setting 1, **at the same height and zoom as the plate photos**)

Plates are named as in `pilot_plates_log.csv` (PILOT-1 to PILOT-4). Put the date and time in the file's properties, or in your notes.

## Every session

1. Same room, same light, same height and zoom setting as the last session. Write the zoom setting down.
2. LEDs turned down so the lid doesn't glare. Tilt the plate slightly if it does.
3. The plate rests flat on a paper towel or tray, **not** on your electronics mat.
4. Photograph in this order: whole plate, centre, then any colonies you want to follow.
5. Photograph a ruler at each zoom setting you used, so sizes can be measured later.
6. Wipe the stage and your hands with alcohol afterwards.

## Notes to write for each plate

- Day and date.
- What you can see: none, specks, small colonies, colonies spreading, fuzzy growth.
- A rough colony count if you can, and the colour of the colonies (**do not try to identify them**).
- Anything odd: condensation, a crack, a leak, growth outside the streak.

## Counting

Use only the day 7 photos for the numbers. Day 3 and 5 are for the record. Have a second person count the same photos without seeing your count. Differences under about threefold are within the noise.
