# Home agar-plate screening of the fridge dispenser

Written for: you and your household, doing a first screening before sending samples to an accredited lab. This is a screening method. It can show whether the fridge water grows noticeably more than the tap, but the numbers are rough and can't stand alone as evidence.

## 1. Safety first (please read this before buying anything)

Growing out water samples multiplies whatever is in them, and some of what could be there (*Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Candida*, *Aspergillus*) can cause infections in vulnerable people.

1. **Incubate at room temperature only (about 20 to 25 °C). Do not use a warm incubator at 30 to 37 °C.** Body-temperature incubation selects for the organisms that grow in people. The lab can do 35 °C for you.
2. **Keep every plate sealed once it's inoculated.** Tape the lid to the base all the way round (or wrap with laboratory film). **Never open a plate after it has grown**, and never sniff it. Fuzzy mould colonies release spores that float in the air.
3. **Keep plates away from food, the kitchen and bedrooms,** somewhere out of reach of children and pets, and away from air vents.
4. **People who are immunocompromised, pregnant, very young or very old should not handle or incubate the plates,** and ideally shouldn't be in the room while you work.
5. **Don't try to identify organisms at home.** Count and photograph only. If you see something you want identified, the lab can do it.
6. **Dispose of plates safely** (section 7).
7. Wash hands before and after, wear disposable gloves, and don't eat or drink while working.

If you have a lung condition, asthma triggered by mould, or take immunosuppressant medicine, ask someone else to do the plating.

## 2. What to buy

- **R2A agar plates** (90 mm), pre-poured, or R2A powder plus empty plates if you can prepare and sterilise media. R2A is the standard low-nutrient medium for water bacteria. Ordinary nutrient agar underestimates water bacteria and isn't a substitute.
- **A fungal plate**: Sabouraud dextrose agar with chloramphenicol, or DG18 agar. These favour yeasts and moulds. (Mould and yeast will also appear on R2A, but the fungal plate is clearer.)
- **Sterile water or sterile saline** in sealed single-use ampoules or bottles, for dilutions (pharmacy "sterile water for irrigation" works).
- **Sterile syringes (1 mL) or sterile pipettes**, a fresh one per sample and per dilution.
- **Sterile disposable spreaders** (or a sterile loop). Don't reuse them.
- **Sterile swabs** for the nozzle.
- **Sterile bottles** (100 to 250 mL) for water samples. Boil-sterilised glass jars are second best.
- **Surface disinfectant** (70% ethanol or isopropanol), tape or laboratory film, a marker, gloves, a ruler and a camera.
- **A cheap DPD chlorine test kit** for tap and fridge water.

Supplies for laboratory use are sold by scientific suppliers; some sell to individuals, and some will need an institution's account. If you can't buy plates, skip to the lab and use this protocol's controls and sample design for it.

## 3. The samples (same as the test plan)

Use the visit and round structure in `test_plan.md`. At each visit collect:

| Code | Sample |
|---|---|
| T | Kitchen cold tap after 2 minutes' running |
| F1 | Fridge first draw, after 8 or more hours' non-use |
| F2 | Fridge after flushing 2 litres |
| N | Nozzle swab |
| D | Drip tray swab (optional) |
| B | Field blank (sterile bottle opened and closed at the fridge, then treated like a sample) |

Process within **2 hours** of collection, keeping bottles cool (fridge temperature) in between. Sample the **old filter first** and don't clean or sanitise anything beforehand.

## 4. Plating water samples

Work at a cleaned bench (wipe with 70% alcohol), with windows and fans off, and gloves on. Label every plate on the **base** (not the lid) with the date, round, visit, code and volume plated.

**Dilutions** for F1 and F2 (the contaminated-looking ones): mix 1 mL of sample into 99 mL of sterile water to make a **1 in 100** dilution, then 1 mL of that into 9 mL sterile water for **1 in 1,000**. Use a fresh syringe each time and mix by gentle swirling.

**Per sample, plate these (spread plate):**

| Sample | R2A plates | Fungal plates |
|---|---|---|
| T (tap) | 1 mL undiluted, in duplicate | 1 mL undiluted |
| F1 and F2 | 1 mL undiluted; 0.1 mL of 1 in 100; 0.1 mL of 1 in 1,000 | 1 mL undiluted; 0.1 mL of 1 in 100 |
| B (blank) | 1 mL undiluted | 1 mL undiluted |

To spread: put the volume on the plate, then spread across the surface with a sterile spreader until it's evenly wet, and leave the plate lid-on, upright, for 15 minutes to absorb before inverting. For a 1 mL load, spread gently and let it absorb for longer (up to 30 minutes) before inverting.

**Drip tray swab (D, optional):** the same, across the grille and the surface under it, as found and before any cleaning, on one R2A and one fungal plate. Never open the plates afterwards.

**Nozzle swab (N):** rub the swab round the inside rim of the outlet, then stroke the swab across one R2A and one fungal plate in a zigzag over the whole surface. No counting; just note growth and photograph.

**Controls (every session):**
- **Sterility control**: one R2A and one fungal plate from the same batch, sealed and incubated without being opened at all. Any growth means the plates were already contaminated, and the session's results can't be trusted.
- **Air control**: one R2A plate left open next to your work area for the whole session, then sealed. It shows how much the room contributes.
- **Blank (B)**: described above.

**Incubate:** seal with tape, invert (lid down), stack no more than 3 high, **20 to 25 °C, in the dark**, for **7 days**.

## 5. Reading and counting

- **Check on days 3, 5 and 7.** Photograph each plate each day, against a dark background with a ruler in shot and the same lighting. **Don't open them.**
- **Count through the lid** with a marker dot on the lid for each colony counted, or count from the photo.
- **Use the 7-day count** (the bacteria are slow). Day 3 and 5 counts are for the record.
- **Only plates with about 30 to 300 colonies give a reliable count.** For tap water with few colonies, a count of 1 to 29 is an estimate; report it as such. If a plate has more than 300 colonies, report the next dilution instead.
- Have a **second person count the same plates** from the photos without seeing the first count, then average. Record both.
- Mould colonies are fuzzy and often coloured; yeast colonies are smooth, creamy and domed; bacterial colonies are shiny and range from beige to yellow to pink. If you're not sure, record "unsure". Don't identify further.
- Swarming colonies that spread across a plate: count as 1 and note "spreading".

**Calculation:**

```
CFU per mL = colonies counted ÷ volume plated (mL) × dilution factor
```

Example: 47 colonies on the plate that received 0.1 mL of the 1 in 100 dilution gives 47 ÷ 0.1 × 100 = **47,000 CFU per mL**.
If no plate is countable, give a range: for example more than 300 colonies on the 1 in 1,000 plate means **more than 3 × 10⁵ CFU per mL**.

## 6. How to read the results

These are **R2A counts at room temperature after 7 days**, a closer match to a standard heterotrophic plate count at 20 to 28 °C than to Payment's 35 °C counts. Don't compare them directly to his numbers.

| Result | What to conclude |
|---|---|
| Sterility control or blank shows growth | Throw out that session's results; the technique or plates were contaminated |
| Fridge counts similar to tap (within about 3 times) | No sign of regrowth in this fridge at this time |
| Fridge F2 at least 10 times the tap, on at least two of three visits | A real difference. Worth sending to the lab |
| F1 much higher than F2 | Stagnation in the line |
| High and similar in F1 and F2, lower after the filter change | Points to the filter |
| Fungal growth in N but not in F2 | Outlet surface issue |
| Fungal growth in F2 | In the water itself. Send to the lab for identification |

Home counts are rough, with counting error of perhaps ±30% even at good counts, so treat only large differences (10 times or more) as meaningful. **Whatever you find, confirm with an accredited lab before saying anything publicly,** and don't claim the plates show harm. They show exposure.

## 7. Disposal

1. Keep every plate taped shut. **Don't open them.**
2. Put the plates in a heavy-duty bag and seal.
3. The best way to kill them is a **pressure cooker at full pressure for 30 minutes** (a home autoclave) with the sealed plates in a heat-safe container; let it cool fully before opening the cooker. Then bin them.
4. If you don't have a pressure cooker: put the sealed plates in a bucket, cover with **10% household bleach** (one part bleach to nine parts water) for **at least 30 minutes** in a ventilated place, with the lid taped still. Then bin them. Bleach penetrates a taped plate slowly, so leave them longer (overnight) if you can.
5. Wash and disinfect the bench and wash your hands.

## 8. Schedule for one visit

| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| 0 | Collect samples, plate within 2 hours, seal, incubate. Chlorine test at tap and fridge |
| 1 | Check the sterility control only, without opening |
| 3, 5, 7 | Photograph and note counts |
| 7 | Final count, calculate CFU per mL. Dispose of all plates (section 7) |

Use `plate_log_template.csv` to record counts, and keep every photograph with the plate code in the filename.

The `build/` folder has an offline counting tool (`build/index.html`) that counts colonies from your photos, does the CFU/mL arithmetic, applies the rules above, and keeps the log. Its accuracy has only been tested on computer-made plates, so check it against your own manual counts first (see `build/README.md`).

## 9. Then what?

If any fridge sample shows a clear difference from the tap, collect fresh samples for an accredited lab using the plan in `test_plan.md`, ideally on the same days. Ask the lab for R2A heterotrophic plate counts at 35 °C and 20 to 28 °C, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *E. coli* and coliforms, and yeast and mould. Your home plates become the first draft; the lab report is what you'd show on screen.
