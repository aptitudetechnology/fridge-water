The Hygiene Trap

Fridge water is the last stretch of plumbing nobody audits. Measured: in ten homes, fridge-dispenser water carried thousands of times more bacteria than the tap feeding it. Our hypothesis: over years that harms people, and bad breath may be one of the first signs. Here is what's known, what it could cost, and how anyone could find out.

Watch the animation

What this is

A script, a set of animated sketches with narration, the evidence behind them, and a plan for testing fridge water ourselves with agar plates. The story is about how an everyday appliance could hide in plain sight, and about the human cost when a cause goes unseen.

It is not a campaign against filter makers or the people who service them. Chlorination is one of public health's great successes. The question is narrower: what happens in the last metre of pipe, after the filter has taken the chlorine out?

Where the evidence stands

Evidence tiers

Established

  • Bacteria colonise carbon filters and plumbing.
  • A 2024 study of ten homes found far more bacteria in fridge water than in the tap feeding it, with the chlorine gone.
  • Opportunistic microbes matter most for people with weak immunity.

Plausible

  • Taste may change slowly over months.
  • Home filter units at high bacterial counts went with more gut illness in a 1991 trial.
  • In one person, one tap-water bacterium persisted in the gut for weeks (2022).

Untested

  • Years of drinking from an ageing dispenser: any effect on a healthy gut, inflammation or ageing?
  • No direct study that we could find.
  • Our hypothesis: years of it can show as bad breath, through hydrogen sulfide in the water, the tongue and the gut. Plausible, and untested in fridge water.
  • Whether low-dose microbes could even be protective is an open question we are researching too.

Every citation is checked against its record, and the notes say how far each has been read. Some AI research reports we received had invented or mislabelled references. Those are marked do not cite in the files.
